Workflow Detective · pilot guide for companies

Find the busywork your team could stop doing

Workflow Detective quietly notices the steps your people repeat at their computers, like copying the same details from emails into a spreadsheet, and turns them into a plain-English list of what could be automated. Nobody has to describe their job or know anything about AI. This guide covers what we ask of your company, what your participants do, and what the tool can and can’t see.

What we ask of your company

  1. Choose 1 to 3 people who spend a lot of their day on admin: operations, bookkeeping, accounts payable or receivable, scheduling, customer support, order processing. People who say “I’m always copying things between systems” are ideal.
  2. Give the OK in writing. A reply to our email is enough. Each participant also agrees for themselves on their setup page, and taking part is always voluntary.
  3. Send us their names, and if you know them, whether each person uses a Windows PC or a Mac. We send each person their own setup email.
  4. Let them use their normal work computer. Not a personal device.
  5. Join the readout in week 3 if you’d like. You see the findings each participant chose to share, never their activity.

What participants do

Each person gets an email with their personal link. The link already knows their name, company and schedule, and the page works out what kind of computer they have, so they only see the steps that apply to them.

  1. Open the link on their work computer.
  2. Click the big button. Either Add to Chrome/Edge (for work done in websites) or Download for Windows/Mac (for programs like Outlook and Excel), sometimes both. The page explains any warning their computer shows along the way.
  3. Read what it records, tick I agree, and click Start the study. Their details are already filled in. With the browser extension, Chrome or Edge then asks them to allow access to websites, and they click Allow.
  4. Work as usual for two weeks. A small magnifying-glass icon shows it’s running, and they can pause it any time.
  5. On the last day, they open My findings with us, confirm which findings are right, and choose whether to send us the results file.

If someone gets stuck, they can open their link again. It shows which steps are done. Or they can reply to the email and we’ll help.

What it can and can’t see

What it notices

  • Which program or website is in use, and for how long
  • Window and page titles, with numbers and email addresses removed
  • That something was copied or pasted, and what kind of thing it was (a number, a date). Never what it was.
  • The names of fields filled in, like “Customer”. Never what was typed.
  • That a file was downloaded or saved, and its type (like .csv). Never the file.

What it never sees

  • What anyone types, or the contents of emails, chats, documents and spreadsheet cells
  • Screenshots or recordings of the screen
  • Passwords, card numbers, bank details
  • Private browser windows, password managers, banking and health sites, or any app the person blocks
  • Anything outside watch days and work hours
  • Everything stays on the participant’s computer. There are no servers. Nobody, including you and us, can see the data while it’s there.
  • The participant reviews everything first, can delete any part of it, and decides whether to send us their results file at the end.
  • It isn’t a performance tool. Findings describe processes (“copying invoice details from email into the accounting system”), not how fast or how much someone works.
  • Automatic deletion: data on the computer is deleted 14 days after the study ends. We delete results files 30 days after the pilot unless you ask us to keep them for an automation project.

Questions

Does anyone have to change how they work?

No. The point is to see normal work. Participants should do exactly what they’d do anyway.

Will it slow down their computer?

No. It only writes down a small note when something changes (a different app comes to the front, a copy happens), and keeps them in a small file.

Can a participant pause or stop?

Yes, any time. Pause for an hour from the icon, block any website or app, or stop completely, which deletes everything it recorded.

What do we get at the end?

A short list of the repetitive processes it found for each participant who chose to share, how often they happen, roughly how much time they take each week, and a suggested way to automate each one, from a simple template to an integration between systems.

What if someone works mostly in websites, or mostly in desktop programs?

Both work. Their setup page asks one question and picks the right version: a browser extension for websites, a Windows or Mac app for programs, or both.

Does it use AI on our data?

No. Findings come from simple rules that run on the participant’s own computer, so their activity is never sent to an AI service or anywhere else.
