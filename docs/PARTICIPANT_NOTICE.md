# Workflow Detective Pilot: What You're Agreeing To

**Company:** [Company name]  **Participant:** [Name]  **Dates:** [Mon start date] to [end date]  **Pilot contact:** Tejas Senthil, tns976@eid.utexas.edu

## What this is

Your company is taking part in a 2-week pilot to find repetitive work that could be automated, like copying the same details from one app into another or filling in the same form every day. You don't have to describe your job. A browser extension (and, for some people, a small Windows or Mac app) notices the steps you repeat and lists them in plain English. You review the list before anyone else sees it.

## When it runs

- 3 days a week for 2 weeks. Week 1: Monday, Wednesday, Friday. Week 2: Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday.
- Only between [8:00 am] and [6:00 pm].
- Only in Chrome or Edge on your work computer. Never in private/incognito windows.
- You can pause it for an hour, block any website, or stop completely at any time.

## What it records

- Which work app you're using and for how long, for example "QuickBooks, Invoice page".
- Page titles, with numbers, email addresses, and phone numbers removed. You can turn this off.
- That you copied or pasted something, and what kind of thing it was (a number, a date). **Not what it was.**
- The names of form fields you fill in, like "Customer" or "Due date". **Not what you typed.** You can turn this off.
- That you downloaded or uploaded a file, and its type (like .csv). **Not the file.**
- The names of buttons you click, like "Save and send".

## What it never records

- What you type, or the contents of emails, chats, and documents
- Passwords, card numbers, bank details, Social Security numbers, or other sensitive fields
- Screenshots or recordings of your screen
- Anything outside the browser
- Social media, banking, and health websites, or any site you block

## If you're using the Windows app

Some participants use a small Windows app instead of, or as well as, the browser extension, so it can see desktop programs like Excel, Outlook, and QuickBooks Desktop. Everything above still applies, with these differences:

- It sees **every app on your computer** during watch hours, not just the browser: which program and window is in front, and window titles like a file name or email subject, with numbers and email addresses removed.
- In **Excel**, it records which sheet and which column headings you edited (like "Amount"). Never what's in the cells.
- In **Outlook**, it records the subject of the email you're looking at, with numbers removed. Never the email text, sender, or recipients.
- It notices when a new file appears in Downloads, Desktop, or Documents, and its type (like .xlsx). **Never the file's name or contents.**
- It **never watches the keyboard** and never takes screenshots. It doesn't need administrator rights.
- Password managers, banking and health sites, and any app or site you add to the never-watch list are skipped.
- Its icon sits near the clock. Right-click it to pause, see your findings, or stop. Stopping deletes everything it recorded.

## If you're using the Mac app

Same as the Windows app above, with these differences:

- macOS asks you to allow **Accessibility** for Workflow Detective. That's what lets it see window titles and which field you're typing in. It still never sees what you type.
- It **never reads what you copy**. It only notices that you copied something and what type of thing it was (text, cells, a file).
- It notices new files in your **Downloads** folder only, by type.
- In Excel it sees the workbook name, not sheets or column headings. It doesn't record which buttons you click.
- Its icon is the magnifying glass in the menu bar at the top of your screen.

## Where your data goes

- It stays **on your computer** during the study. Nobody, including your employer and the pilot team, can see it.
- You can open **My findings** any time to see the findings and the full activity log, and delete any line, a whole app, or everything.
- At the end, **you decide** whether to send a results file to the pilot team. It contains the findings and your ratings. It includes your activity log only if you tick that box.
- Everything on your computer is deleted automatically 14 days after the study ends. The pilot team deletes results files 30 days after the pilot unless your company asks to keep them for an automation project.

## Your choices

Taking part is voluntary. You can pause, block sites, delete data, or stop at any time with no consequences. To stop, open the extension's **Settings and privacy** page and click **Stop and delete everything**, then remove the extension.
